# Service card — Measured pilot, productized

> **Illustrative.** One student's rung-2 pilot from the [Module 20 offer ladder](../../module-20/solution/workshop-kit/offer-ladder.md), rewritten as a repeatable service after three deliveries. Clients are fictional. Prices are placeholders of a plausible size (USD, as of 2026-09). Format: [service card template](../../../templates/service-card.md). Check with `ScaleCheck service`.

- Service: Measured pilot for one legacy .NET and SQL Server team
- Version: 2.0.0
- Price: USD 18,000 fixed
- Duration: 8 weeks, one team of 5–10 developers, one repository
- Planned days: 10
- Hours per day: 8
- Floor: 1,500 per day (see pricing worksheet, 20.2)
- Entry: application scored CALL; named sponsor; 30+ tickets a month; baseline exportable from the tracker; coding agent already approved by the client's security team
- Playbook: engagement playbook, pilot chapter (Module 21)

## Steps

| # | Step | Owner | Standard asset | Planned hours | Custom |

| 8 | Client-specific questions and follow-ups | me | - | 4 | yes |

## Not included

- More than one repository or one team; a second team is a new pilot or rung 3.
- Changes to the client's CI, production systems or production data.
- Selecting or buying the coding-agent tool, or negotiating with the vendor.
- Any productivity target. The pilot measures; it does not promise a result.
- Work for my employer's customers, competitors or suppliers (admin register, 20.5).

## What changed from v1

- Kickoff, audit and report use templates written after the first delivery instead of blank documents.
- The AI layer starts from the practitioner kit instead of from scratch (the biggest saving: 38 h in D1, 20 h planned).
- Custom work is a named, budgeted step (16 h) instead of something that leaks into every step.
- Duration grew from 6 to 8 weeks so that the comparison has enough tickets; my days fell from 12 to 10.
