# Instructor notes — 17.4 Rehearsal protocol

**Teaching objective.** Students plan and run three rehearsals with different jobs (solo technical run, friendly run with a drilled failure, dress rehearsal with outsiders), log them, split timing drift into controlled and estimated parts, get two independent observer scores on the anchored rubric, calibrate large disagreements, and judge the workshop against an exit standard written in advance. `KitCheck rehearsal` says READY.

**Likely confusion.** "Three rehearsals means three run-throughs." The three have different jobs because each measures something the others cannot. Second confusion: total drift as the timing measure. Positive and negative errors cancel; the split into controlled and estimated drift is the useful number.

**Common misconception.** "I know the material, so rehearsal is about polish." Rehearsal is about the parts that are not the material: timing with real people, laptops, fallbacks, questions, and how often you look at your notes under pressure. Second: "observers are for beginners." Experienced trainers use observers because they cannot see their own silent gaps.

**Key analogy.** A theatre production's technical rehearsal, preview and opening. The tech run checks lights and cues with nobody in the seats; previews put a real audience in front of an unfinished show so the director can change it; opening night is what the critics see. No producer counts three tech runs as previews.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Dress rehearsals with friends who helped build the kit. Observers who score together and converge. Notes checks estimated from memory instead of counted on the recording. Logs without a "changes made" section, so rehearsals repeat the same mistakes. Lowering the standard after rehearsal 3 because the date is fixed.

**Expected exercise outcome.** Three logs in the template format, each with timing, incidents, scores (from rehearsal 2 on) and changes; a dress rehearsal before at least three outsiders with two independent observers, notes checked at most twice, drift within five minutes and no criterion below 3; a `LearnCheck gain` run on the dress rehearsal forms. For the break: the six failed gates and the +17/−13 drift split that the +4 total hid.

**Extension exercise.** Record the dress rehearsal and have a third person, who was not in the room, score it from the recording. Compare their scores with the two live observers: which criteria are visible only in the room, and which are clearer on video?

**Discussion question.** The exit standard says "notes checked at most twice". What does that number measure, what does it miss, and would you keep it as a standard for a trainer's tenth delivery of the same workshop?
