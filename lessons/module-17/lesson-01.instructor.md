# Instructor notes — 17.1 The workshop agenda

**Teaching objective.** Students turn their Module 16 session and Module 15 run sheet into a 2-hour agenda: the six-part arc, blocks of a short live segment followed by the same step as hands-on on a sibling ticket, a wait-time plan per segment, a named buffer, a break, a three-minute close, a written cut order, and cue-card instructor notes. `KitCheck agenda` and `LearnCheck align` both pass.

**Likely confusion.** "A 2-hour workshop is four 30-minute sessions." It is one arc with one repository, one method and a running story; the blocks share the before clip, the credibility and the Q&A. Second confusion: students treat Q&A during wait-time as active learning. It is valuable, but it is passive for everyone except the asker; the prediction question comes first because everyone answers it.

**Common misconception.** "The demo is what people come for, so it should be most of the time." People come for what they can do on Monday; the demo is the worked example. Second: "the 10-minute rule is about attention span." Bradbury (2016) found no good evidence for a fixed limit; the rule exists to force practice, and the lesson says so.

**Key analogy.** A cooking class. The chef does one step at the front (dice the onion), then every station dices its own onion, then the chef walks round. Nobody learns by watching the chef cook the whole meal for an hour and then being handed a knife as the kitchen closes.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Timestamps written after the minutes and never re-added, so a row drifts. Live segments with a fallback on the run sheet but not in the agenda. A buffer hidden in generous step lengths instead of its own row. Instructor notes that are paragraphs copied from the lesson.

**Expected exercise outcome.** An `agenda.md` with three or four objectives, two items each, 15–20 rows, both checks at 0 errors, the first hands-on before 0:45, live segments at most ten minutes each with a fallback and a prediction question, a written cut order, and `instructor-notes.md` with one section per row under 150 words. For the break: the timestamp error and the 2:01 end, the 4% hands-on and 54% demo, the missing fallbacks and buffer, and a rewrite close to the reference.

**Extension exercise.** Write a 90-minute remote version of the same agenda. Which block do you drop, how do the hands-on steps work in breakout rooms (16.4), and what does `LearnCheck align` say about passive stretches when you shorten show segments to five minutes?

**Discussion question.** The recipe's 15-minute close with an offer made sense for a public workshop that funds the trainer. When, if ever, is an offer in a workshop that a company paid for acceptable, and who should decide?
