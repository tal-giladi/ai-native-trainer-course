# Instructor notes — 21.5 Writing the anonymized case study

**Teaching objective.** Students write a one-page case study from the engagement record in which every number, interval and status is traceable, anonymized by estimating how many organizations match the description, reporting what is generally expected rather than the best case, with limitations as a section and written client approval.

**Likely confusion.** Pseudonymization vs anonymization: replacing "Contoso" with "C-01" is the former; the latter is judged by whether a motivated intruder could re-identify. Second: why the FTC endorsement guides are relevant to B2B consulting: not as binding law for every reader, but as a clear statement of the principle that a showcased result is read as typical.

**Common misconception.** "An inconclusive result is not publishable." It is, and it builds trust with technical buyers. Second: "If the checker is clean, it is anonymous." The Haifa sentence passes the checker and identifies the client.

**Key analogy.** Publishing a bug report from a customer incident: you strip the customer, the hostnames and the account IDs, and you also think twice about the one-of-a-kind configuration that makes it obvious which customer it was.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Rounding in the student's favour (−8% becomes "about 10%"). Keeping the exact headcount because "19 is not identifying". Leading the results with the most flattering row and moving the inconclusive ones below the fold.

**Expected exercise outcome.** A one-page case study in the template's shape, `EngageCheck casestudy` clean against the record and a private deny list, a written $k$ estimate in the hundreds, and a dated approval (from the peer playing the sponsor). Break: the Haifa sentence passes the checker; students estimate $k$ falling from hundreds to about 1.

**Extension exercise.** Ask someone who knows your local market to play the motivated intruder on your draft for five minutes. Record what they guessed and which detail gave it away.

**Discussion question.** A prospect says, "Your case study says the delivery result was inconclusive. Why should we hire you?" Answer in three sentences.
