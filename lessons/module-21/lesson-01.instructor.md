# Instructor notes — 21.1 Discovery, kickoff and stakeholder mapping

**Teaching objective.** Students turn a signed SOW into a working engagement: a stakeholder map built from past-event interviews before kickoff (including security or IT and the business users of the output), a named client acceptor per deliverable, dated access, data handling, a premortem with client owners, and a success measure that is a measurement with a stated detectable effect.

**Likely confusion.** How this differs from the discovery call in 20.3 (that qualified the client; this maps the people inside it) and from the 19.1 stakeholder map (that planned a rollout over months for hundreds; this protects a 12-week engagement where one missing person costs a week). Second: "influence" means ability to stop, slow or accept the work, not seniority.

**Common misconception.** "The sponsor will tell me who matters." Sponsors name the people they manage; they rarely name the controller in Finance or the security reviewer, because those people are not in their reporting line. Second: "Acceptance by the team is more inclusive." It is unaccountable.

**Key analogy.** A production change request. You would not deploy without knowing who approves, who is on call, and which downstream systems read the table you change. The kickoff record is the change request for the engagement itself.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Interviewing with opinion questions ("Would you support…?") and getting uniformly positive answers. Second: premortem risks owned by the consultant. Third: writing the detectable effect as a target ("we aim for 38%").

**Expected exercise outcome.** A kickoff section with nine stakeholders, six named acceptors, dated access (2026-10-05), a data-handling line matching SOW section 13, at least three premortem risks with early signals and client owners, a success measure without targets. `EngageCheck kickoff` clean. The break exercise: without the security lead, access slips to about week 4 (three-week review started in week 1); without the controller, BILL-97 is blocked in the 2–4 November freeze.

**Extension exercise.** Interview one real person at your own organization with the four questions about an AI tool introduced there. Write the answer to question 4 in their words and decide whether that person belongs in the "high influence" row.

**Discussion question.** The sponsor asks you not to involve the security lead "because he slows everything down". What do you say, and what do you write in the record?
