# Instructor notes — 19.3 Enablement mechanics

**Teaching objective.** Students design role-based training paths measured beyond reaction, run office hours and a feedback loop whose outputs change the AI layer, size the loop with Little's law, assign every recurring mechanism to an employee owner, and tie enablement to the acceptable-use policy and the AI-literacy obligation.

**Likely confusion.** Little's law needs a stable system: it describes averages when capacity exceeds arrivals. When $\mu < \lambda$ there is no steady $L$, only growth. Second: "owner" means the person accountable for the mechanism happening, not the person who attends it.

**Common misconception.** "Enablement is training." Training is one of five mechanisms, and the one that decays fastest without practice time, office hours and a loop that visibly fixes things. Second: "a busy channel means an engaged community." A channel with no triage is an unanswered queue with an audience.

**Key analogy.** An incident process. You would never run production with "post in the channel if something breaks": there is an intake, a triage owner, severity classes, a time-to-response target and a postmortem that changes the system. Feedback about the agent deserves the same, at a smaller scale.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Owners who are the student's own name "for now". Second: an arrival rate pulled from the air with no plan to measure it. Third: a developer path that is the Module 17 workshop without the practice blocks, so behaviour never forms.

**Expected exercise outcome.** A section 3 with five paths, measures beyond level 1 for developers at least, a timed setup path, six rhythms with employee owners and calendar slots, $\rho \le 0.8$ with hours agreed, `enablement` clean. Break: the three changes add at least three errors and a growing backlog of about 4 items a week.

**Extension exercise.** Draft the two-page acceptable-use policy with the headings: allowed uses, data that may not enter prompts, review requirements, where to ask, what is logged and who sees it. Have one person from legal or security mark it up.

**Discussion question.** A client wants to save money by replacing office hours with an internal chatbot trained on the FAQ. What would the loop lose, and under what conditions might it still be a good idea?
