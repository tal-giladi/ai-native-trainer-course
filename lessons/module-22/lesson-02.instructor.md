# Instructor notes — 22.2 Workshop to course to community

**Teaching objective.** Students choose their next format from their own evidence, build a catalog with honest live days and revenue ranges, license every artifact (software licence for code, CC for documents, written terms for paid and team use), keep a TASL register, and write community rules. `ScaleCheck catalog` clean.

**Likely confusion.** Licence vs ownership. Students think licensing is about protecting against copying; it is about telling people what they may do. Ownership is already decided by creation and by their employment contract and SOWs (20.5). Second: "CC is for everything". Point to CC's own FAQ on software.

**Common misconception.** "A course is a recorded workshop." The room is the pedagogy (16.3); a recording loses it. The course needs its own exercises, checkpoints and measured gain. Second: "A community is passive income." It is fixed live days a month plus moderation; with "direct access to me" it is a retainer sold to everyone at once.

**Key analogy.** Open-source packaging. You would not publish a library without a LICENSE file, a README, a version and a list of vendored third-party code with their licences. A course or kit is a package; the catalog is its manifest.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Units per month copied from the dream, not from the funnel (18.1); ask for the source of every high number. Second: using a client's anonymized workshop materials "because names are removed"; the SOW decides, not the anonymization. Third: team licences with no term.

**Expected exercise outcome.** `catalog.md` with 3–5 products, each with a licence and its own evidence (or "none yet"), live days and a forecast range; a licence table with at least the public code, public documents, method names and any paid material; a third-party register; community rules if applicable; `catalog` clean.

**Extension exercise.** Draft the one-page team-licence terms as a buyer would receive them. Ask a lawyer or an experienced buyer in your market for 15 minutes of comments, and list the questions they asked that your draft did not answer.

**Discussion question.** A learner who bought your course posts one of its exercises, with credit, in a public blog post. Is that a problem? What does your licence say, and what would you want it to say?
