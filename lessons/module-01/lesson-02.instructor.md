# Instructor notes — 01.2 Engineering pain vs AI hype

**Teaching objective.** Students classify requests as pain, hype, symptom or blocker with four tests plus the workaround signal, size one real pain with effort (engineer-hours, range) kept separate from flow (median, p90) and bounded by capacity, and map buyer, users, champion and blocker.

**Likely confusion.** Symptom versus pain. "Low seat usage" feels like a pain because it is a number and a VP is unhappy. Ask: "If the number went up tomorrow, would anyone's week be better?" If you cannot say whose, it is a symptom. Blocker versus pain confuses people too: the CISO is not suffering; she is protecting.

**Common misconception.** "Hype is bad and should be ignored." Hype marks who has urgency and budget this quarter. The skill is to ask what event triggered it and translate it into a problem the buyer can report upward. A second misconception: "the bigger the sized pain, the better the pitch" — which produces the 3,276-hour slide.

**Key analogy.** A doctor's intake. "I need antibiotics" is a request for a solution; "I've had a fever for four days and tried paracetamol" is a symptom history with a failed workaround. Good doctors take the history before prescribing, even when the patient is the one paying.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Students size a pain by multiplying calendar wait by volume, or give single-point estimates ("45 minutes") that invite argument. Second: converting hours to money. Stop it; money comes after evidence (Module 20). Third: naming stakeholders by person ("Dave") instead of role, which will not transfer to other clients.

**Expected exercise outcome.** Classifications matching the key on 10+ of 12 (items 5, 11 and 12 cause most disagreement, which is fine if defended); a `pain-sizing.md` with two or three effort terms as ranges, flow as median/p90, a capacity check under 10% for most real pains; a stakeholder map by role; one hype request rewritten with a baseline number and no tool name.

**Extension exercise.** Size the same pain two ways: from data (PR export) and from what a colleague estimates in conversation. How far apart are they, and in which direction? Relate the gap to the METR finding that self-estimates can have the wrong sign.

**Discussion question.** In the worked example the review-queue pain is only 1–3% of capacity as effort. Is it still worth solving? What would you say to a CTO who asks "why should I care about 40 hours a month?"
