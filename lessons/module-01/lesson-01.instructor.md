# Instructor notes — 01.1 The roles and the flywheel

**Teaching objective.** Students pick one role to lead with (trainer, consultant, enablement engineer), place themselves on the four-stage flywheel, and write a proof chain: six client sentences, each tied to an artifact and to the module that produces it. They leave knowing why the course is ordered the way it is.

**Likely confusion.** Trainer versus consultant. Both "run workshops". Ask what the client is paying *for*: people who can do something (trainer) or a metric that moves (consultant). The deliverable and the success measure follow from that. Enablement engineer confuses people who have never worked in a platform team; describe it as "the person who owns the AI layer after the consultant leaves".

**Common misconception.** "Skill is the bottleneck." Most students are already skilled. The bottleneck is checkable evidence and the order in which it is produced. A second misconception: that public teaching is marketing to be delegated later. It is the cheapest test of whether you can explain.

**Key analogy.** A surgeon's case log. Nobody hires a surgeon for being passionate about surgery; they ask how many of this procedure you have done and what happened. The proof chain is the case log you have not written yet.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Students list artifacts that only they can see ("I have a great prompt library on my laptop") or sentences about themselves ("I'm an expert in AI"). Insist on: someone outside your team can open it today; the sentence is about the client's outcome. Second: marking a row "have" when it is "partly have"; ask them to show it.

**Expected exercise outcome.** A `role-and-stage.md` with one lead role and one thing given up; a strict artifact list (often empty or one item); six client sentences mapped to modules, nearly all "missing", with one circled as the first a staff engineer would attack. The only row they can finish this month is interview evidence.

**Extension exercise.** Find a trainer or consultant in this space whose work you respect. Reconstruct their flywheel from public traces: when did the first talk appear, the first repo, the first case study, the first paid offer? What evidence preceded each? What do you *not* know about them?

**Discussion question.** The flywheel is modelled on someone who succeeded. What would you expect to see in the careers of people who tried the same path and stopped, and which stage do you think most of them stopped at?
