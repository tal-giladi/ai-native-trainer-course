# Instructor notes — 16.3 Show, do, reflect

**Teaching objective.** Students turn aligned objectives into a timed show-do-reflect plan: short modelling that narrates decisions, faded exercises with a finish line and a hint ladder, a live step with checkpoints, a prediction question and a fallback, and a retrieval-based reflection. `LearnCheck align` passes, and a rehearsal with a timer matches the plan.

**Likely confusion.** "If the demo is the most impressive part, shouldn't it be longest?" The demo is the most impressive part *for the presenter*. Second confusion: students count "Q&A" as active time. Unless every learner must produce something, it is passive for all but the one asking.

**Common misconception.** "Live coding shows authenticity, so it should be unscripted." Authenticity comes from real code and real failures, not from improvisation. The script card, the tags and the fallback are what let you stay calm when it breaks, which is the most authentic moment you will have (Module 15). Second: "desirable difficulty means making exercises hard." It means making learners generate before they see, with support available.

**Key analogy.** A driving lesson. The instructor drives one lap, thinking aloud (show); the learner drives with the instructor's hands near the wheel (guided practice, hint ladder); then alone on a new road (independent practice); then they talk about the junction that went wrong (reflect). Nobody learns to drive by watching a longer first lap.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Handout exercises without done-criteria ("look at the diffs"), so pairs finish at wildly different times and the debrief has nothing to compare. Second: live steps that depend on network and model latency without a recording. Third: the rehearsal skipped because "I know the material"; the timer always disagrees.

**Expected exercise outcome.** An activities table passing `align` with at least 40% active time and no passive stretch over ten minutes; a handout with complete exercise specs and hint ladders; a live-step card with a prediction question; tagged checkpoints and a tested recording; one rehearsal with real timings noted. For the break: the 19-minute passive run, 14% active and the two unpractised objectives identified, and a rewrite close to the reference.

**Extension exercise.** Rewrite one of your *show* steps as a *do* step: instead of modelling the first diff, give learners the diff and the search output and ask them to decide first, then model your reasoning as feedback. Run both versions with two different people and compare their answers on the matching post-test item.

**Discussion question.** Some engineers say they learn best by watching an expert stream for hours. What is true about that, and what would you need to see before designing a session around it?
