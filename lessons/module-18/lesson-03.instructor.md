# Instructor notes — 18.3 Content formats

**Teaching objective.** Students choose formats by what each proves and costs, keep short pieces to one concept, size scripts from word counts, and publish at least two formats for one concept, including a captioned, problem-first video on `brownfield-demo` at a pinned tag, checked with `PostCheck format` and `claims`.

**Likely confusion.** Why the tool ignores lines starting with `[`: they are screen directions, and the time they take (a pause on a diff, an agent run) must be planned separately. Second: "one concept" does not mean "one fact"; a concept card has a claim, evidence and a boundary, and all three belong in the piece.

**Common misconception.** "Polished production matters most." Guo et al. found informal talking-head segments and brisk, enthusiastic speakers more engaging than polished studio recordings, and the one-take habit from 15.3 is itself evidence. Second: "Longer videos show more expertise." Median engagement stayed around six minutes whatever the length; the extra minutes are mostly unwatched.

**Key analogy.** Commit size. One logical change per commit makes review and revert possible; one concept per piece makes a piece shareable and answerable. A three-concept video is a 2,000-line commit: nobody reviews it.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Recording on `main` instead of a tag, so the video diverges from the repository within a week. Second: accepting auto-captions without reading them; ask students to count the errors they corrected. Third: showing a terminal with an employer's hostname in the prompt or a notification from a work chat.

**Expected exercise outcome.** One concept published in two formats (usually a post and a short video), both passing `format` and `claims`, with the calendar updated. For the break: all five problems named (length, three concepts, captions, employer repository, presenter-first opening) and the split plan into three videos.

**Extension exercise.** Time your own speaking rate on a two-minute rehearsal and set your personal words-per-minute constant. Rerun `format` mentally with it: which of your scripts would change target?

**Discussion question.** A conference offers you a 40-minute slot to present your whole method. What would you put in it, what would you leave out, and which parts would you point to in other formats instead?
