# Instructor notes — 18.1 Content as a funnel

**Teaching objective.** Students plan public content as a system with a purpose: pieces placed on discover, trust and act stages, each teaching one concept from `concepts.md` with its evidence, at a cadence they can sustain, measured by unprompted questions from outside their network rather than by reach. They leave with a calendar that passes `PostCheck calendar`.

**Likely confusion.** "Funnel" suggests marketing, and engineers either reject the word or overcorrect into marketing. Make clear the funnel here is a model of how trust accumulates, and that its output is a question, not a sale. Second: "outside" and "unprompted" are two separate conditions; students often count a stranger's question at a meetup Q&A (outside, prompted) as the exit signal.

**Common misconception.** "More reach means more credibility." Reach mostly measures the 90% who never react, and the pieces with the biggest reach are often the ones furthest from the method. Second: "I need to be an expert before I publish." The student is already the only person with their dated incidents; first-hand experience is the asset.

**Key analogy.** Monitoring a service. Reach is like request count: easy to collect, says little about whether users succeed. Outside questions are like a synthetic check that exercises the real path: rare, specific, and they tell you something works end to end.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Planning pieces around topics ("AI agents and testing") rather than concepts with evidence. Second: choosing an unsustainable cadence (three a week) and a burst in week one. Third: leaving the outside-questions column out because the platform does not show it; it has to be counted by hand.

**Expected exercise outcome.** A `talks/content-calendar.md` with an audience sentence, two or three places with evidence, a cadence, and six planned pieces on supported concepts, none of them asks, passing `calendar --concepts` without errors. For the break: all four groups found (no concept or evidence, 50% asks, 42-day silence, reach without signal) and a fix close to the reference.

**Extension exercise.** Take two public figures in your wedge whose content you trust. Classify their last 20 pieces as discover, trust or act, and count how many teach one checkable idea. Compare with your plan.

**Discussion question.** A hot take would reach ten times more people than your careful article. Under what circumstances, if any, would you publish it, and what would you need to be true for it not to cost you credibility?
