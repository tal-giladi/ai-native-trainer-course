# Instructor notes — 18.4 Lunch-and-learn, free pilot, and questions into content

**Teaching objective.** Students run two low-stakes live reps (an internal lunch-and-learn and a free pilot run like a paid engagement), log every question anonymized and themed, turn every theme asked three times into a public piece, and recognize and confirm the exit signal with `PostCheck questions`.

**Likely confusion.** Where the pilot sits between Module 16's mini-workshop and Module 21's engagements. Answer: it is the mini-workshop, delivered to an outside host under engagement discipline (written scope, consent, follow-up), and its write-up is a short public piece, not the full case study. Second: which questions go in the log. All of them, including prompted and inside ones; only the exit signal needs outside and unprompted.

**Common misconception.** "A free pilot doesn't need paperwork." It needs more, because nothing else defines what was agreed; the free pilot that ends in an unapproved post is a lost reference. Second: "Answering in DMs is more personal." It is, once; the fourth identical DM is a piece you have not written.

**Key analogy.** A support queue. Tickets answered only in chat are lost knowledge; a recurring ticket becomes a knowledge-base article, and the next ticket is closed with a link. The questions log is your queue, the pieces are the knowledge base.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Writing questions down days later from memory, which loses the wording and half the questions; ask students to open the log before the session. Second: logging names. Third: choosing a pilot host at the student's employer or a client of it, which makes it an employer activity (14.4). Fourth: publishing the pilot write-up before the host has approved the final draft.

**Expected exercise outcome.** `talks/lunch-and-learn/` with speaker notes and feedback including a follow-up; `talks/pilot/` with agreement notes, plan and results; `questions.csv` with anonymized, themed rows; at least four published pieces; `questions` without errors. Most students will not have the exit signal by the end of the module; that is expected, and the log is set up to catch it.

**Extension exercise.** Go through your questions log and mark every question that is counter-evidence for a concept card. Update the card's boundary or counter-evidence line and, if it changes what you teach, bump the method's version under your evolution policy (14.3).

**Discussion question.** Your manager asks you to run the lunch-and-learn for the whole engineering department and to mention your paid workshop at the end. What do you agree to, and what do you decline?
