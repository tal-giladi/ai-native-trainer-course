# Instructor notes — 14.4 Originality, attribution and intellectual property

**Teaching objective.** Students know, at orientation level, what copyright, trademark and contracts do and do not protect in a method; can tell original synthesis from a copied framework with the four questions; complete an attribution audit (words by tool, shape by hand) against three or more public frameworks and this course; and clear employer material before anything is taught.

**Likely confusion.** "If loops cannot be copyrighted, why credit?" Separate the two questions explicitly: the law answers "can they stop me?", the audience answers "do I trust this person?". This lesson is about the second. Second confusion: students think crediting weakens their offer. In an expert audience it strengthens it: it shows they know the field.

**Common misconception.** "Renaming is enough." A synonym for someone else's idea, uncredited, is still a copy — and easier to catch, because it looks deliberate. Second misconception: "the course's words are mine now, I paid for the course". The course deliberately used working names and told students to replace them.

**Key analogy.** Academic citation. A paper that uses a well-known method cites it in one line and then spends its pages on what is new. Nobody thinks less of it for the citation; everyone thinks less of a paper that presents the known method as new.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Choosing three obscure frameworks so the audit is clean. The frameworks must be ones the student's audience knows. Second: skipping the shape audit because the word audit passed. Third: employer check done as "I'll anonymize later". Ask for section 4 filled before the first public talk (Module 18).

**Expected exercise outcome.** An extended `terms.csv`; `borrowed` with no open terms; an audit with all five sections filled, including a contribution sentence the student can say aloud; a credits paragraph in `method-v1.md`; web and trademark searches recorded with dates; a paraphrase retest after renaming. For the break: 11 terms found, the copied shape named as the larger problem, and a fix resembling the reference.

**Extension exercise.** Email or message the author of the public framework closest to yours (only if you are comfortable), share your credits paragraph and contribution sentence, and ask whether they are fair. Log the reply in the FAQ. If you do not send it, write the reply you would expect and what it tells you.

**Discussion question.** A former colleague starts teaching your Shadow rule under a new name, without credit, and it is catching on. You have no legal claim. What do you do, and what does your own attribution practice make possible here?
