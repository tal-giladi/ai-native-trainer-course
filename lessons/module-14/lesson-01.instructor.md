# Instructor notes — 14.1 Why vocabulary is the IP

**Teaching objective.** Students can tell a methodology from a tips list, a tool tutorial and a copied framework using the seven parts; they understand that named, bounded, evidenced concepts are what they will be paid for; and they split their draft into a method page and a dated implementation page, verified with the tool-change test.

**Likely confusion.** "If I remove the tool names, isn't the method too abstract to use?" No: the method keeps concrete outputs (a brief with a source per line, a plan with an out-of-scope list, checks the agent cannot edit). What moves out is how to produce them in one product. Show the side-by-side table from Fix it.

**Common misconception.** "Good names are marketing." In this course a name is a handle on a claim with evidence and a boundary; a catchy name without those is a slogan and will diffuse into meaninglessness. Second misconception: that the loop is the IP. The loop shape is common; the concepts from the student's own incidents are what nobody else has.

**Key analogy.** A recipe vs a kitchen appliance manual. The manual ("press Pulse twice") is useful and expires with the model; the recipe ("emulsify before adding the acid, or it splits") survives any blender. Students are writing the recipe and keeping a separate note on which buttons do it on today's blender.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Inventories built from polished documents only, so every term is the course's. Send students to their failure-diagnosis log and retro notes. Second: students "fix" `tooldep` by deleting tool words without rewriting the sentence, leaving "press it twice". Third: problem statements that exclude nobody ("teams using AI").

**Expected exercise outcome.** An inventory of 20–40 terms with origins; typically 60–80% tagged course, public or tool, and 3–6 candidate phrases tagged *mine* with incident IDs. A `method-v1.md` skeleton with seven headings, a problem statement with an explicit non-audience, one boundary, `Version: 0.1.0`. `tooldep` at or below 20%, plus an implementation file. For the break: 84% found, and a rewrite close to the reference's 3%.

**Extension exercise.** Swap skeletons with another student who uses a different agent. Each tries to run one ticket from the other's method using only the method page and their own tool. Every point where they needed the other's implementation file is a sentence that belongs in the method as an output, not a click.

**Discussion question.** A popular vendor publishes a new "official" workflow next month that covers two of your loop steps in its own words. Which parts of your method lose value, which gain, and what do you change on your method page?
