# Instructor notes — 20.3 Qualification and discovery calls

**Teaching objective.** Students build an application form whose every field serves a decision (with conflict and expectation flags, no unnecessary personal data), triage applications into call, nurture, refer and decline, and run a 30-minute discovery call that finds the incident, the success measure, the decision path, budget, timing and constraints, says a limit aloud and ends with a dated next step. Both `qualify` and `call` clean on their own material.

**Likely confusion.** How this differs from Module 1's customer discovery. There, you were researching a wedge and must not pitch. Here, the person has applied and may buy, so the last third of the call is about fit and price. The first two thirds follow the same rules: past events, their words, you listening. Second: whether an application form "signals scarcity". The source material says it can; the course's version only states a real cap, and the form's main job is qualification.

**Common misconception.** "Qualifying people out loses revenue." It loses bad revenue: the client with no sponsor stalls, the conflict client ends your job, the headcount client blames you when nobody is fired. Second: "Pressure tactics are normal in B2B." Some are common; that does not make invented scarcity true. Students who have been on the receiving end usually recognize the list immediately; ask them how it felt.

**Key analogy.** Triage in incident response. Not every alert gets paged to a human; severity and ownership decide. The form is the alert rule, the score the severity, REFER the "not our service, route to that team". Paging everything burns the on-call engineer, which is what a calendar full of unqualified calls does to you.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Writing the call notes afterwards from memory and in the student's words, which inflates their share and loses the quotes; ask for notes taken live with timestamps. Treating the internal pitch as not needing qualification; the sponsor, budget and timing questions matter most there. Improvising a discount on the call because the prospect hesitated; the price comes from the ladder.

**Expected exercise outcome.** `application-form.md` adapted, with a privacy note and a real cap; three test applications scored by `qualify`; one real discovery call written up in `discovery/` with `call` clean or with warnings the student can explain; the promised follow-up sent on time.

**Extension exercise.** Role-play the break call in pairs, with the "seller" instructed to use every pressure tactic in the table and the "buyer" instructed to say what each one made them feel and do. Then swap and run the script. Compare what each buyer disclosed.

**Discussion question.** A friend who runs a small agency applies, scores 2 of 5 (no budget, no timing), and asks for "just a quick call to pick your brain". Do you book it, nurture, or decline, and what do you say?
