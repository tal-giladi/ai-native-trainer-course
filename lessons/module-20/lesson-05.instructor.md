# Instructor notes — 20.5 Legal, admin and difficult clients

**Teaching objective.** Students read their own employment contract's IP, outside-work, non-compete and confidentiality clauses; get or request written permission; keep an admin register with employer-conflict, contract, tax and invoicing items; book professional review; and can point to the document that answers each difficult-client pattern. `OfferCheck admin` shows no blockers before the first external pitch.

**Likely confusion.** What this lesson can and cannot tell them. It gives questions and examples from three jurisdictions; it does not tell anyone whether a clause is enforceable where they live. Say so at the start and again when a student asks "so am I allowed to…?": the answer is "ask a lawyer, with the clause in hand". Second: the internal pitch. Selling to your employer usually is not moonlighting, but the work may then be part of the job, with no invoice and employer-owned output.

**Common misconception.** "If I do it on weekends on my own laptop, it's mine." Often, but not always: broad assignment clauses, work relating to the employer's business, or use of employer knowledge can change that, and rules differ by jurisdiction. Second: "Calling myself a contractor makes me one." Status follows how the work is controlled and paid. Third: "Legal paperwork scares small clients." Clear, short documents reassure them; vagueness is what scares procurement.

**Key analogy.** Dependency licences. Before you ship a product, you check the licence of every dependency, because one GPL file in the wrong place changes what you can do with the whole. Your employment contract is the licence your side work depends on; read it before you ship.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Filling the register from memory of the contract instead of the text. Marking "yes" without evidence. Postponing the lawyer "until I have a client", which is exactly too late for E1–E3. Students outside the US, UK or EU assuming the cited rules apply to them.

**Expected exercise outcome.** Private notes with the four clauses quoted; a written request for permission sent (or a clear internal-only plan); `workshop-kit/admin.md` with every row filled or annotated with who will answer it; a booked professional consultation; `admin` without blockers before any external pitch.

**Extension exercise.** Write a one-page "difficult conversations" sheet: for each pattern in the table, the first two sentences you will say and the document you will point to. Rehearse two of them aloud with a colleague playing the client.

**Discussion question.** Your employer approves outside work "as long as it doesn't use anything you learned here". Your method was built from incidents at this job, anonymized in Module 14. Is that approval enough? What would you ask, and how would you word the request?
