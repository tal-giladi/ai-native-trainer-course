# Instructor notes — 13.6 Running and reporting the comparison

**Teaching objective.** Students run their pre-registered comparison to its planned end, account for every ticket, wait for guardrails to mature, and write a report and one-pager in which every sentence sits at a supported rung of the claims ladder and money appears only as a range that includes quality costs.

**Likely confusion.** Weekly checks vs peeking. Students ask "so I can't look at the data for six weeks?". They can and should look — at completeness, missing timestamps, balance and deviations. What they must not do is compute the effect and let it influence whether, when or how the experiment ends.

**Common misconception.** "A null or messy result is a failed project." For a trainer it is the opposite: a well-run null result is the strongest evidence of integrity in the portfolio, and it tells the method (Module 14) where agents do *not* help. Second misconception: that a report recommending against rollout "wastes" a real speed-up. The worked example is built so that the speed criterion passes and the guardrail fails.

**Key analogy.** An auditor's report: the value is not in finding good news but in the reader being able to trust that bad news would have been reported.

**Common failure in the exercise.** Reports without a ticket flow (tickets quietly disappear: abandoned, re-scoped, still open). Second: guardrails reported the day the last ticket merged. Third: the one-pager drifts one rung up the ladder from the report ("for this team's tickets" becomes "for our teams").

**Expected exercise outcome.** A committed pre-registration with at least one dated deviation (there always is one); a data file with both clocks, `used_ai` and versions; the pre-registered analysis plus 2–4 sensitivity checks; a report following the template with a "does not show" section of 3+ items; a one-pager. For the break: at least eight unsupported claims found and a rewrite at rungs 1–2 proposing a randomized follow-up.

**Extension exercise.** Swap reports with another student. Each marks every sentence in the other's one-pager with its rung on the claims ladder and flags any sentence above rung 3. Revise and compare the before/after one-pagers.

**Discussion question.** Your sponsor dislikes the pre-registered decision ("don't roll out yet") and asks you to "reframe the defect numbers". What do you change in the report, what do you refuse to change, and how do you keep the relationship?
