### C-20261031-01 · ICML 2026 plug-in correction for LLM-judge sensitivity/specificity bias, with combined test+calibration confidence intervals

- **Class:** A
- **Kind:** new-topic
- **Date discovered:** 2026-10-31
- **Date published:** 2026 (ICML 2026 / PMLR vol. 306, pp. 65231–65252; peer-reviewed proceedings, no single preprint date surfaced)
- **Source:** https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/lee26bl.html (fetched and verified this session)
- **Organization/researchers:** Chungpa Lee, Thomas Zeng, Jongwon Jeong, Jy-Yong Sohn, Kangwook Lee (academic; affiliations not shown on the proceedings page)
- **Category:** evaluation
- **What changed:** A peer-reviewed (ICML 2026) paper formalizes exactly the correction the course already teaches by hand — adjusting a naive LLM-judge pass rate for the judge's own sensitivity/specificity error — and adds two pieces the course's current formula does not have: (1) confidence intervals that combine uncertainty from *both* the test set and the human-labeled calibration set (not just a point correction), and (2) an adaptive rule for allocating calibration-labeling effort to tighten those intervals. It also argues their estimator stays unbiased even when the test and calibration distributions differ (e.g., calibrating on one task and judging a harder one).
- **Technical summary:** Quoted from the fetched abstract: "We propose a simple plug-in framework that corrects this bias and enables statistically principled uncertainty quantification" for cases where "imperfect sensitivity and specificity of the LLM judges induce bias in naive evaluation scores." The framework estimates judge sensitivity/specificity from a human-labeled calibration set, plug-in-corrects the naive score, and builds a CI around the corrected estimate that propagates both the calibration set's and the test set's sampling noise — the paper states it "remains unbiased under distribution shift between the test and calibration datasets," unlike (their claim) existing approaches. The exact estimator/formula is in the PDF, not reproduced in the fetched abstract page.
- **Why it might matter:** lessons/module-07/lesson-03.md already teaches this exact problem by name and derives the identical point-correction logic: it defines TPR/FPR from a confusion matrix against 20–50 human labels, derives $q_{\text{measured}} = \text{TPR}\cdot p + \text{FPR}\cdot(1-p)$ (a worked numerical example with TPR=0.583/FPR=0.500 showing two systems 50 points apart scoring only 4 points apart), and ships this as the `EvalHarness calibrate` command's "correction formula." What it does *not* currently do is put a confidence interval on the corrected $p$ itself, or say how calibration-set size trades off against that interval's width — exactly what this paper adds, and exactly the kind of rigor module-10/lesson-04 (error bars on evals, Miller 2024) and module-13 (statistics ladder, CIs, Hedges g) already insist on elsewhere in the course. `lookup "judge calibration"`, `"Apollo Research"`, `"AgentJudgeBench"`, and `"severity rubric"` confirm this specific CI-around-the-correction method is new to the course and registry.
- **Evidence of adoption:** N/A — peer-reviewed methods paper, not a product; no tool/library implementation confirmed from the fetched page.
- **Major organizations using it:** none named.
- **Open-source implementation:** not confirmed from the fetched page (not stated there); check the PDF/repo before use.
- **Paper:** https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/lee26bl.html
- **Code:** unconfirmed.
- **Relationship to existing course material:** lessons/module-07/lesson-03.md (grader precision/recall, the TPR/FPR correction formula, `EvalHarness calibrate`); lessons/module-10/lesson-04.md (error bars on evals, Miller 2024 cited); module-13 statistics lessons (confidence intervals, pooled estimators). Extends, does not contradict, what 07.3 already teaches.
- **Potential course lesson:** not a new lesson; candidate for a short addition to 07.3's calibration subsection — a worked confidence interval around the existing TPR/FPR-corrected pass rate $q$, cross-referenced to module-10/lesson-04's error-bar material — once a future session reads the full PDF for the exact estimator.
