# Module 16 quiz — Instructional design for engineers

Nine scenario questions on designing and measuring a short teaching session: telling reaction from learning, writing observable objectives and items at the right level, managing cognitive load, sequencing show, do and reflect, handling a mixed or skeptical room, and computing and reporting a normalized gain honestly. They test reasoning, not recall: most describe a session plan, a room or a set of results and ask what went wrong or what to do next.

**Covers**

- [16.1 · How adults and engineers learn](../lessons/module-16/lesson-01.md) — Kirkpatrick's levels, reaction vs learning, the fluency illusion, working memory and schemas, ICAP, learning claims.
- [16.2 · Objectives, alignment and cognitive load](../lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md) — backward design, constructive alignment, observable objectives and levels, extraneous load, worked and faded examples, expertise reversal, misconception items.
- [16.3 · Show, do, reflect](../lessons/module-16/lesson-03.md) — modelling and guided practice, live coding with checkpoints and prediction questions, exercise specs and hint ladders, retrieval at the end, pacing.
- [16.4 · Managing the room](../lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md) — question triage, wait time, psychological safety, mixed levels, skeptics, remote rooms.
- [16.5 · Measuring learning](../lessons/module-16/lesson-05.md) — normalized gain and change, Cohen's d, bootstrap intervals, measurement threats, feedback next to learning, behaviour follow-up.

**Pass mark:** 70% (7 of 9). Unlimited retries; answers and explanations appear after you submit.

Before you start, it helps to have run the checks in [`labs/module-16`](../labs/module-16/README.md) on the break files.
