- **Positioning** — One sentence naming one audience, one problem in the buyer's words, the alternative they use today, what you do and how they will know it worked (lessons/module-20/lesson-01.md)
- **Jobs to be done** — Framing a purchase as hiring something to make progress in a particular circumstance; the job, not the method, is what a buyer pays for (Christensen et al.) (lessons/module-20/lesson-01.md)
- **Offer ladder** — Three to five fixed-price offers (e.g. workshop, measured pilot, implementation, retainer) in which each rung produces the evidence the next one needs and a buyer can stop after any rung with a useful result (lessons/module-20/lesson-01.md)
- **Decoy option** — An offer added mainly to make another option look better by comparison (the asymmetric-dominance effect); excluded from an honest ladder (lessons/module-20/lesson-01.md)
- **Cost floor** — The day rate below which work makes you poorer: target income plus business costs divided by genuinely sellable days (lessons/module-20/lesson-02.md)
- **Value range** — A client's possible monthly net gain computed from your own measured effect interval, the quality guardrail's cost, adoption share and tool spend, reported as an interval rather than a single figure (lessons/module-20/lesson-02.md)
- **Probability of loss** — The share of Monte Carlo draws in which the client's net value over the horizon, after your fee, is negative; stated in the proposal when material (lessons/module-20/lesson-02.md)
- **Loaded rate** — The full hourly cost of an employee to the employer: wages plus benefits and overhead, not salary alone (lessons/module-20/lesson-02.md)
- **Qualification** — Deciding, honestly and early, whether there is a real problem, a sponsor, budget, timing and no conflict, and answering every applicant with call, nurture, refer or decline (lessons/module-20/lesson-03.md)
- **Data minimization** — Collecting only the personal data a stated purpose needs; on an application form, name and work email (lessons/module-20/lesson-03.md)
- **Discovery call** — A 30-minute call with an applicant that establishes a past incident, a success measure, the decision path, budget, timing and constraints before any offer is fitted to it (lessons/module-20/lesson-03.md)
- **Pressure tactic** — A sales move that pushes a decision through invented scarcity, deadlines, bandwagon claims, leading questions or bypassing the decider rather than through information (lessons/module-20/lesson-03.md)
- **Statement of work (SOW)** — The document that binds one option: deliverables as results with acceptance criteria, exclusions, assumptions, client responsibilities, measurement, fees, change control and legal clauses (lessons/module-20/lesson-04.md)
- **Acceptance criterion** — A condition for accepting a deliverable that a third person could check, unlike "to the client's satisfaction" (lessons/module-20/lesson-04.md)
- **Change request** — A written request to change scope, schedule or deliverables that states its effect on fee and schedule and is approved before work on it starts (lessons/module-20/lesson-04.md)
- **Scope creep** — Work outside the SOW done without a change request or logged decision; silent scope creep is the most common source of consulting disputes (lessons/module-20/lesson-04.md)
- **Background IP** — The method, templates, tools and materials you owned before an engagement; a SOW keeps them yours and licenses what is embedded in the client's deliverables (lessons/module-20/lesson-05.md)
- **Work made for hire** — In US copyright law, a work owned from creation by the employer or commissioning party: an employee's work within the scope of employment, or a commissioned work in one of nine categories with a written agreement (lessons/module-20/lesson-05.md)
