- **Kirkpatrick levels** — Four levels of a training's effect: reaction (was it favourable and relevant), learning (did they acquire the skill), behaviour (do they apply it at work), results (did organizational outcomes change); a high score at one level says little about the next (lessons/module-16/lesson-01.md)
- **Fluency illusion** — The feeling of having learned something from watching it done smoothly; shows up as self-rated confidence far above the post-test score (lessons/module-16/lesson-01.md)
- **ICAP** — Chi and Wylie's classification of engagement as Interactive, Constructive, Active or Passive, predicting learning in that order (lessons/module-16/lesson-01.md)
- **Learning claim** — One sentence naming who, in which situation, will be able to do what observable thing after a session (lessons/module-16/lesson-01.md)
- **Backward design** — Planning a session from desired results to assessment evidence to activities, in that order (Wiggins and McTighe) (lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md)
- **Constructive alignment** — The verb in the objective is the verb the learner performs in the activity and in the assessment (Biggs) (lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md)
- **Cognitive load** — Demand on limited working memory; intrinsic load comes from the material, extraneous load from the design and can be removed (lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md)
- **Expertise reversal effect** — Guidance that helps novices, such as worked examples, becomes redundant load for more experienced learners (lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md)
- **Faded worked example** — A sequence from a fully worked example to a half-solved one to an independent problem, removing support step by step (lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md)
- **Misconception item** — An assessment item whose distractors are misconceptions heard from real learners, often including a correct-looking non-example (lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md)
- **Prediction question** — A question learners answer before a result appears (for example during an agent run), turning watching into generating (lessons/module-16/lesson-03.md)
- **Hint ladder** — Graded hints for an exercise, from a nudge to the near-answer, so that struggle ends in success rather than guessing (lessons/module-16/lesson-03.md)
- **Wait time** — The pause after asking a question before answering it yourself or moving on; longer pauses produce more and better answers (lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md)
- **Psychological safety** — A team's shared belief that it is safe to take interpersonal risks such as asking questions or admitting errors (Edmondson) (lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md)
- **Normalized gain** — Class-average improvement divided by the maximum possible improvement, (post − pre) / (100 − pre); Hake's bands: low below 0.3, medium 0.3 to 0.7, high 0.7 and above (lessons/module-16/lesson-05.md)
- **Normalized change** — Per-learner gain, bounded for losses as well as gains, averaged over learners; undefined at 0% or 100% on both tests (Marx and Cummings) (lessons/module-16/lesson-05.md)
- **Parallel forms** — Two versions of a test with the same items on different surfaces, given in counterbalanced order before and after a session (lessons/module-16/lesson-05.md)
- **Ceiling effect** — Items or tests most learners already pass before teaching, which therefore cannot show learning (lessons/module-16/lesson-05.md)
