- **Content funnel** — A model of public teaching in three stages: discover (a stranger meets one idea), trust (they can apply it and come back) and act (a piece asks for something); asks are kept to at most one piece in five (lessons/module-18/lesson-01.md)
- **Participation inequality** — The pattern that most members of online communities only read, a few contribute occasionally and very few contribute most; summarized by Nielsen as the 90-9-1 rule (lessons/module-18/lesson-01.md)
- **Outside question** — A question from someone who did not know you before, asked without being prompted; the signal a content funnel is measured by, as opposed to reach or likes (lessons/module-18/lesson-01.md)
- **Source credibility** — The effect of who is seen to say something on how persuasive it is; stronger at first than later, which is why lasting credibility rests on checkable content (Hovland and Weiss) (lessons/module-18/lesson-01.md)
- **Substantiation** — Having a reasonable basis for an objective claim before making it; an express claim of support such as "studies show" needs at least that support (FTC) (lessons/module-18/lesson-02.md)
- **Limits line** — The sentence or section in a public piece that says where the claim does not apply and what the evidence does not show (lessons/module-18/lesson-02.md)
- **Disclosure line** — A statement in the piece itself of any material connection to what it discusses, such as an employer, a vendor, free access or payment; "none" when there is none (lessons/module-18/lesson-02.md)
- **Repurposing** — Teaching one concept in several formats (post, article, video, talk, case-study piece), each adding a kind of proof the others cannot carry (lessons/module-18/lesson-03.md)
- **Free pilot** — A free, short workshop for an outside host run exactly like a paid engagement, with written scope, a plan, level 1–3 measures and written consent before any write-up (lessons/module-18/lesson-04.md)
- **Questions log** — A record of every audience question with channel, network, prompted, anonymized asker, theme and where it was answered; a theme asked three times becomes the next piece (lessons/module-18/lesson-04.md)
- **Exit signal** — Module 18's exit test: the first unprompted follow-up question from someone outside your network (lessons/module-18/lesson-04.md)
