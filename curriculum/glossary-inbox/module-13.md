- **Cycle time** — Time from a ticket entering In Progress to its change being merged; the default speed metric for comparisons because its clock starts before the way of working can act (lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md)
- **Lead time for changes** — DORA metric: time from a change being committed to it running in production (lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md)
- **PR review time** — Time from a pull request being opened to being merged; useful for the review queue, not comparable across ways of working that open PRs at different points (lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md)
- **Rework** — Fix commits on the same files within a fixed window after a merge (21 days in this course), counted per ticket (lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md)
- **Escaped defect** — A bug traced to a ticket after it was deployed, within a fixed window (30 days in this course) (lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md)
- **DORA metrics** — DORA's five software delivery metrics: change lead time, deployment frequency and failed deployment recovery time (throughput); change fail rate and deployment rework rate (instability) (lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md)
- **Geometric mean** — The mean on the log scale transformed back, exp(mean of ln x); the natural summary for skewed durations, and the basis of "percent faster" as a ratio (lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md)
- **Quality-adjusted throughput** — Tickets per period that met a stated quality bar (for example no rework and no escaped defect within the windows) (lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md)
- **Cost per successful task** — (Human hours × loaded rate + agent and tooling spend) ÷ tasks that met the quality bar (lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md)
- **Evidence ladder** — Ordering of study designs from lab RCTs through field RCTs, quasi-experiments and surveys to vendor claims, by what each can show about cause and transfer (lessons/module-13/lesson-02.md)
- **Perception gap** — The difference between how much faster developers believe a tool makes them and what is measured; METR's 2025 trial found a 20% perceived speed-up alongside a 19% measured slowdown (lessons/module-13/lesson-02.md)
- **Control arm** — A concurrent comparison group doing the same kind of work at the same time without the treatment (lessons/module-13/lesson-03.md)
- **Randomization** — Letting a random number, not a person, decide which unit gets the treatment, so the arms are comparable on measured and unmeasured factors (lessons/module-13/lesson-03.md)
- **Confounder** — A variable that influences both who gets the treatment and the outcome, such as ticket size when developers choose when to use an agent (lessons/module-13/lesson-03.md)
- **Blocking (stratified randomization)** — Randomizing separately within groups fixed before assignment (for example developer × size) so the arms are balanced on them and the analysis can remove their variation (lessons/module-13/lesson-03.md)
- **Unit of randomization** — What is randomly assigned: a ticket, a developer or a team; it sets the sample size and the spillover risk (lessons/module-13/lesson-03.md)
- **Pre-registration** — A committed, dated plan of question, arms, metrics, analysis and decision rule written before any outcome is seen; later changes are logged as deviations (lessons/module-13/lesson-03.md)
- **Statistical power** — The probability that an experiment detects an effect of a given size if it exists; sets how many tickets per arm are needed (lessons/module-13/lesson-03.md)
- **Post-treatment variable** — A variable measured after the treatment could affect it (for example lines changed); never block, stratify or adjust on it (lessons/module-13/lesson-03.md)
- **Threats to validity** — Ways a study can produce a difference not caused by the treatment (internal), measure the wrong thing (construct) or fail to transfer (external) (lessons/module-13/lesson-04.md)
- **Regression to the mean** — The tendency of a unit selected for an extreme value to move back toward its usual level on the next measurement, with no intervention (lessons/module-13/lesson-04.md)
- **Hawthorne effect (research participation effect)** — A change in behaviour caused by being studied or observed rather than by the treatment (lessons/module-13/lesson-04.md)
- **Contamination** — Units in the control arm receiving the treatment (or treatment units skipping it), which dilutes the measured effect (lessons/module-13/lesson-04.md)
- **Intention to treat (ITT)** — Analyzing units by the arm they were assigned to, whatever they actually did; preserves randomization and answers the policy question (lessons/module-13/lesson-04.md)
- **Per-protocol analysis** — Analyzing units by the treatment they actually received; reintroduces selection, so used as a sensitivity check (lessons/module-13/lesson-04.md)
- **Difference-in-differences (DiD)** — The treated unit's change over time minus the comparison units' change over the same time; valid when the treated unit would otherwise have moved in parallel (lessons/module-13/lesson-04.md)
- **Placebo test** — Applying the same analysis to units that were not treated, to see how extreme the real result is compared with ordinary variation (lessons/module-13/lesson-04.md)
- **Bootstrap confidence interval** — An interval from the spread of an estimate recomputed on many resamples of the data drawn with replacement (lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md)
- **Permutation test** — A test that shuffles treatment labels many times to see how often a difference as large as the observed one arises when labels are arbitrary (lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md)
- **Effect size** — A measure of how large a difference is (ratio of geometric means, Hedges g, Cliff's delta), as opposed to how surprising it is (lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md)
- **Hedges g** — The mean difference divided by the pooled within-group standard deviation, with a small-sample correction (lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md)
- **Cliff's delta** — The probability that a random value from one group exceeds one from the other, minus the reverse; an assumption-free effect size (lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md)
- **Practical significance** — Whether an effect is large enough to matter for a decision, judged against a minimum effect stated in advance; distinct from statistical significance (lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md)
- **Claims ladder** — Levels of claim from observation, effect in this study, effect for this team, effect elsewhere, to money, each requiring more evidence than the last (lessons/module-13/lesson-06.md)
